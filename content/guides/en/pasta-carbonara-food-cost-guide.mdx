## Kitchen notes

Independent restaurants usually under-estimate sauce, oil, and garnish. Cost every gram that leaves the pass — including complimentary bread if you treat it as a menu cost centre. Recalculate when a supplier swaps brands; a “same” oil at a different density changes usable grams per dollar.

Track waste separately from recipe yield. Trim loss belongs in the ingredient yield field; plate scrap and over-portioning belong in a weekly waste log. Mixing the two makes your food cost % look worse than the recipe truly is, and you will raise prices for the wrong reason.

## Supplier rhythm

When the case price moves, edit the ingredient once and let propagation refresh every dish. That is the difference between a living cost system and a spreadsheet you dread opening on Monday. Keep a dated price history so you can explain margin shifts to ownership without guesswork.

Seasonality matters for produce-heavy dishes. If avocado or berries spike for six weeks, either rewrite the garnish, float a temporary surcharge, or accept a higher food cost % with eyes open — but never leave last year’s price in the file.

## Activation checklist

1. Enter pack size and purchase unit exactly as invoiced.
2. Set yield from a real trim test, not a guess.
3. Confirm menu price is HT or TTC consistently with workspace settings.
4. Compare food cost % to your target band for this restaurant type.
5. Export the profitability workbook before a menu reprint.

These steps keep programmatic guides from becoming doorway pages: each dish still needs a unique costed table, a calculator path, and enough operator context to act.
